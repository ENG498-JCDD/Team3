## Analyzing the Stats

According to the [Wake County Food Security Program](https://www.wake.gov/departments-government/wake-county-food-security-program), over 126,000 Wake County residents are food insecure, including roughly 33,550 children. 51% of these residents don't qualify for SNAP benefits, including 41% of food insecure children. Additionally, according to the [Food Bank of Central & Eastern North Carolina](https://foodbankcenc.org/hunger-impact/county/wake), food insecurity impacts 1 in 8 Wake County citizens, including 1 in 4 Black citizens, 1 in 5 Hispanic citizens, and 1 in 11 White citizens. 

These stats suggest that half the county's hungry population automatically can't utilize the SNAP program for food support due to ineligibility, raising questions about how SNAP impacts the 49% who do qualify. The stats also point to demographics like children (of low-income families) and Black people as being among those who need food support most, which prompted us to examine these specific groups when synthesizing the stats with our SNAP findings. 

## Connecting the Stats to Our SNAP Data

Although 49% of Wake County's food insecure population qualify for SNAP, the data we collected reveals that a very small percentage actually receive it. While over 61,000 food insecure people qualify for SNAP in Wake County, [less than 30,000 (roughly 6%) are recipients of the program.](https://www.wxii12.com/article/north-carolina-snap-cuts-county-household-map/69206304) In addition, although the stats gathered indicate that food insecurity is a major issue among children and specific racial groups, the SNAP data we collected shows that these groups aren't necessarily receiving SNAP as much as their level of need calls for. 

The census data we collected on Wake County's SNAP recipient demographics illustrated that in all townships but Raleigh, of the most common type of household with children (single mothers), the vast majority were non-SNAP recipients; this was also the case with the second-most common type of household (two-parent families). Despite over 33,500 children in Wake County being food insecure, Wake County households with children are overwhelmingly non-SNAP recipients, indicating a potential gap between the needs of this demographic and the support they're receiving. 

Additionally, while stats indicate 25% of Black people in Wake County are food insecure, the NC ACS data shows that the number of Black SNAP recipients dropped by almost 7,000 between 2012 and 2022. Despite Black people being disproportionately affected by food insecurity compared to other races, they have experienced by far the biggest drop-off in SNAP enrollment, with White people being the only other racial group to experience a (much smaller) drop in participants. Comparing the NC ACS SNAP data to the Wake County food insecurity stats indicates a sizeable gap in support for Black households, similar to the gap in support for households with children.
